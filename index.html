Linux Exploration

Speed and Style on Low-End PCs

What I investigated: How Different Linux Disctributions and configuration affect performance and usability on low-end hardware.

What I Learned: The experience depends largely on what the user needs. A distribution like Linux Mint can be kept lightweight and minimal, or configured with more features and applications, which can significantly change how it performs and feels on the same hardware.

Linux Distributions For Different Purposes

What I Investigated: How different Linux distributions are suited to different use cases.

What I learned: I'm still exploring this area, but I've found that different distributions can be better suited to purposes such as gaming, programming, system administration, and learning more about how Linux systems work beneath the surface.

Android & Custom ROM Experimentation

Reviving and Modifying an Older Android Device

What I investigated: How an older Android device can be modified and brought back to life using custom recovery software and alternative Android operating systems.

What I did: I experimented with a Xiaomi Redmi 3S, including installing a custom recovery, working with its partition structure, and exploring the process of moving from its original MIUI software to a custom Android ROM.

What I learned: I gained practical experience with Android recovery environments, ROM installation, device partitions, compatibility between software components, and the importance of matching ROMs, recovery tools, and device configurations correctly. The process also showed me how much deeper mobile operating systems go beyond the interface that users normally interact with.

Local AI Research

What I investigated: The possibility of running AI models locally rather than relying entirely on cloud-based services, particularly on hardware with limited memory and processing power.

What I learned: Local AI performance depends heavily on available RAM, processing power, storage, model size, and the software used to run the model. I explored different approaches to running AI locally and investigated which types of models and tools could realistically work within limited hardware constraints.

AERIS — Early Engineering Research

What I Want to Explore

AERIS is an early-stage engineering concept exploring a different approach to personal flight and transportation. The broader goal is to investigate whether personal transportation could become more accessible, flexible, and capable of reaching places that conventional transportation cannot easily reach.

What I Investigated

I began researching existing personal flight systems and the engineering challenges involved in creating a compact flight system. One of the questions I explored was whether energy could potentially be generated from substances available in the surrounding environment, and whether such an approach could realistically provide enough power for flight.

What I Learned

My research showed me that the biggest challenges are not simply about creating movement. Energy density, power requirements, propulsion, weight, stability, control, environmental conditions, and human safety all become major engineering constraints when attempting to create a compact flight system.

Where I Am Now

AERIS is still in the research and concept stage. I am continuing to investigate the underlying science and engineering before attempting to design or build a physical system. My current goal is to understand what is physically possible, identify the major constraints, and eventually develop the concept into a technically grounded engineering project.
